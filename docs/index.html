    The eleven tools and their rules

    1. Chart scanner

    • About every 30 minutes in market hours it screens a fixed list of 46 large US companies and 15 big ETFs, then runs the pack's opening-range breakout scan on the best 20. Crypto: the top-10 coins, without stablecoins, wrapped tokens or meme coins.
    • No penny stocks, options, borrowing or shorting.

    2. News monitor

    • Reads the last 12 hours of Yahoo Finance headlines for every candidate and holding (the pack's news tool).
    • Only headlines that name the company or coin count. Words like beat, upgrade, contract or approval are good news; miss, downgrade, lawsuit, recall or probe are bad news. Two bad headlines, or one serious one, sell a holding.

    How a pick is scored (out of 100)

    Technical: trend, momentum, RSI, volume, near a 20-day high30
    News: today's headlines (12 when there is none)25
    Fundamental: fixed at 9 (no free source; large companies only)15
    Macro: market mood (risk-on 15, cautious 8, risk-off 0)15
    Micro: liquidity, unusual volume, earnings date15

    3. Entry time

    • Buys only with a score of 70 or more (80 when the market is cautious) and one of three setups: a pullback in an uptrend, a breakout (the pack's opening-range breakout, or a new 20-day high on heavy volume), or a news move holding its gains.
    • Never chases: no buying anything already up more than 10% on the day. At most 2 buys a day and 6 a week.

    4. Exit planner

    • Every trade has a stop-loss and a profit target set before buying, the target twice as far as the stop. Breakouts use the pack's exit planner.
    • They act like orders waiting at a broker: if the price touched either one between runs, the trade is counted as sold there. If a bar opened below the stop, it is sold at that worse price.
    • At 3:20 PM, stock day trades are closed unless they are in profit with the trend intact, and stocks are never held into an earnings report (or overnight at all when the earnings calendar can't be read).
    • Stops and targets are checked on 5-minute prices at every run, so one hit at 11:05 is recorded at 11:05.
    • Nothing is held longer than 5 trading days (10 days for crypto). Once a trade is up by its risk, the stop moves to the buy price.

    5. Time machine

    • Before every buy it replays the trade 10,000 times: the pack's replay of the rest of the day on 5-minute bars for breakouts, otherwise the last 100 days of daily prices.
    • It buys only if the replays favour the target. A setup that loses money over 8 trades is switched off.

    6. Risk manager

    • Every order gets 16 pass/fail checks, plus the pack's own risk check for stocks. One failed check blocks it. Selling is never blocked.
    • Down 2% in a day or 5% in a week: no more buys. At $85 all buying stops and you get an alert.

    7. Budget manager

    • Risks 1% of the account per trade, about $1. $70 for stocks and ETFs, $30 for crypto; up to 3 stock or ETF positions and 2 coins.

    8. Order executor

    • Paper fills only, with real costs: 1.5% to convert CAD to USD, 0.1% slippage per stock trade, 0.8% per crypto trade. The program contains no broker code at all.

    9. Price alerts

    • When a good candidate is not yet at a clean entry, an alert is set where it would become one. Checked at every run (every 15 minutes in market hours); up to 6 at once; each lasts 5 trading days.

    10. Earnings analyst

    • Reads Nasdaq's free earnings calendar: no buying in the 2 trading days before a report, nothing held through one, and a profit miss blocks buying for 5 trading days.

    11. Congress tracker

    • Once a day it lists new trade reports from the US House Clerk, with links. It never affects a decision.

    Before real money

    • 20 trading days on paper with no broken rules, and a worst drop under 15%. Then you compare it with buy-and-hold and decide. Twenty days shows the machine works; it cannot prove skill.
