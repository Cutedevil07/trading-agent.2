It runs by itself on GitHub's free computers: every 5 minutes while US markets are open and every 30 minutes otherwise. No cost.

Time machine: what past trades taught it

After every closed trade the agent scores it in R, where 1R is the amount it planned to risk. Results appear here once trades close.

    How it decides

    1. Chart scanner: the whole market

    • Every market morning before the open it reads the chart of every US-listed stock (about 6,000) plus the 300 most-traded ETFs, keeps the ones that trade at least $20 million a day at $5 or more, and picks 60 buy ideas, 40 short ideas and the 30 most active names.
    • Every 10 minutes from 9:50 AM to 2:55 PM it rescans those: opening-range breakouts with the Day Trader pack, and the mirror-image breakdowns for shorts.
    • Crypto: the 100 largest coins, without stablecoins, wrapped copies, meme coins or exchange tokens. Futures: micro contracts on the S&P 500, Nasdaq, Dow, Russell, gold, silver, oil, bitcoin, ether and the euro. The ETF list is refreshed weekly; leveraged and inverse funds are never used.

    2. News monitor

    • Every headline from the last 12 hours that names the company or coin is read by a free AI model trained on financial news, which judges it good, bad or neutral.
    • Bad news helps a short and hurts a buy; good news the opposite. News that breaks the reason for a trade closes it.

    How an idea is scored (out of 100), for a buy or a short

    Technical: trend, momentum, RSI, volume, near a 20-day high (buy) or low (short)30
    News: the AI model's reading of today's headlines25
    Fundamental: sales and profit growth, price/earnings, debt, free cash flow, analysts' target (strong companies for buys, weak ones for shorts)15
    Macro: market mood (rising markets favour buys, falling markets favour shorts)15
    Micro: liquidity, unusual volume, earnings date, Congress buying or selling15

    3. Entry time

    • Needs 70 points when the market mood favours the direction, 80 when it doesn't (75 for gold, oil and the euro), plus one of eight setups. Buys: a pullback in an uptrend, a breakout, a good-news move, strong earnings. Shorts: a rally in a downtrend, a breakdown, a bad-news move, weak earnings.
    • Never chases a move already over 10% today. At most 4 new trades a day and 12 a week.

    4. Options

    • A stock idea scoring 80 or more may use options instead of shares: a call for a buy, a put for a short, 2 to 6 weeks to expiry, near the money, with real open interest and a tight bid-ask spread (Nasdaq's free options data).
    • It only buys options, never sells them, so the most it can lose is what it paid. Sold if the option loses half its value, doubles, the stock hits its stop or target, 5 days before expiry, or before an earnings report.

    5. Exit planner

    • Every trade has a stop-loss and a profit target set first, the target twice as far as the stop. They act like orders at a broker: each run reads every 5-minute price since the last check, so a stop hit at 11:05 is recorded at 11:05 at the stop price, or worse if the price jumped past it.
    • At 3:20 PM, stock day trades that aren't in profit with the trend intact are closed, and no stock or option is held into an earnings report. Nothing is held more than 5 trading days (10 for crypto and options). Once a trade is up by its risk, the stop moves to the entry price.

    6. Time machine

    • Before every trade it replays it 10,000 times: the pack's replay of the rest of the day on 5-minute bars for breakouts and breakdowns, otherwise the last 100 days of daily prices. It trades only if the replays favour the target.
    • A setup that loses money over 8 trades is switched off.

    7. Earnings analyst

    • Nasdaq's calendar gives every report date. No new trade in a company within 2 trading days of its report.
    • After a report it reads profit per share against analysts' forecast, sales growth, and the tone and outlook of the company's own press release from the SEC (or the earnings headlines). Beat, mixed or miss: a miss blocks buys for 5 days and allows shorts; a beat does the opposite.

    8. Risk and budget

    • Each trade risks 1% of the account (about $100). All open trades together may risk at most 6%.
    • One stock or ETF position: at most 25% of the US-dollar money. One coin: at most 40% of the crypto money. One options trade: at most 5% of the account. One futures position: margin at most 30%, and only if one contract risks no more than 1%.
    • Shorts set aside 150% of their value as collateral and pay a 5% yearly borrowing fee. Down 2% in a day or 5% in a week: no new trades. At 85% of the start, all new trades stop.
    • Every order gets 17 pass/fail checks, plus the pack's own risk check for stocks. One failure blocks it. Closing a trade is never blocked.

    9. Price alerts and Congress

    • When a good idea isn't yet at a clean entry, an alert is set where it would become one, and checked every run.
    • Each day it reads every new House trade report. A member's purchase (or sale) in the last 45 days adds up to 3 points to a buy (or a short). Never a reason on its own.

    10. Paper only

    • Fills use real prices and real costs: 1.5% to change CAD to USD, 0.1% slippage on stocks, 0.8% on crypto, the options ask and bid plus $1 a contract, $1.25 a futures contract each way. There is no broker code at all.
    • Shorting, options and futures as tested here need a margin account and approvals that a small real account wouldn't have.
    Paper results use real market prices and modelled costs. They are not real trades and not financial advice. Past results do not predict future ones. Market data from Yahoo Finance and Nasdaq (free, unofficial, delayed).
